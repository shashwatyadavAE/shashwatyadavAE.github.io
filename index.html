Robust Control Framework for Retractable Tether Multirotor UAVs

Jan 2025 – Present · Dynamics and Control Lab, University of South Carolina

Overview

Developing a robust control framework for Retractable Tether Multirotor UAVs (RTMUAVs), tackling the unsolved problem of maintaining stability through slack-to-taut tether transitions under real-world disturbances. This work has sharpened my expertise in nonlinear dynamics, adaptive control, and high-fidelity simulation.

System Modeling

  • Built a fully coupled dynamic model of the UAV, tether, and payload as a unified system capturing nonlinear interactions across both tether states
  • Tether modeled under slack and taut conditions using a spring-damper model and multilink representation
  • State-switching logic with adaptive gains to handle transition dynamics that destabilize conventional controllers

Controller Design

  • Geometric controller with adaptive gains validated in MATLAB against wind and measurement disturbances
  • Implemented Low Frequency Learning (LFL) filter — outperforms standard MRAC in tracking precision while requiring less control rate effort
  • Python simulation environment mapping expected flight envelopes to safety/clearance regions

Disturbance Modeling

  • 8-tone gust field applied throughout testing
  • Sensor noise included in tension feedback term
  • Randomized simulations extracting 95th-percentile excursion envelopes across tether lengths of 2–6 m

Reeling Platform

  • Novel reeling system under 500–800 g tracking tether tension and direction in real time
  • Two-axis gimbal with rotary sensors; reel-rotation sensor for deployment and retrieval tracking

  • 2nd Place · IMECE 2025 ASME Undergraduate Expo
  • Magellan Mini Undergraduate Research Grant — 2025

CFD Study of Crosswind / Yaw Effect on F1 Aerodynamics

Jun 2026 – Oct 2026 · Personal Project

Overview

Five OpenFOAM simulations across 0–8° yaw angles on a CAD model built to 2026 F1 regulations, studying how crosswinds affect downforce, drag, and aerodynamic center.

CAD Model

  • Built in CATIA Generative Shape Design to 2026 F1 regulations
  • Focused on underbody, front wing, and rear wing for downforce and drag accuracy
  • Cross-referenced against comparable models to validate geometry

CFD Setup

  • All simulations in OpenFOAM; car velocity 80,000 mm/s
  • Constant velocity magnitude held across yaw angles to conserve dynamic pressure
  • Rotating MRFs on all four wheels
  • kOmegaSST turbulence model for vortex development and flow separation under yaw

Results

  • CL and CD both increase with yaw angle; CD increases slightly faster
  • Slight downward drift in L/D ratio across the range
  • Crosswinds up to 8° have minimal effect on overall L/D; primary effects on yawing moments and aerodynamic center
